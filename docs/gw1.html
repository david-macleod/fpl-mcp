06 Decision process

Eleven transfers that cost nothing

The API reported transfer status `unlimited` with a null limit: before the first deadline every transfer is free, so the default squad was rebuilt outright rather than nursed. Spend lands on £100.0m exactly with £0.0m in the bank.

Transfers must be position-matched, so the rebuild runs as eleven like-for-like swaps: both keepers, four defenders, four midfielders and one forward. Tarkowski, Rice, Thiago and João Pedro were kept.

Why the fixture horizon changed the squad

A first pass built for Gameweek 1 alone tripled up on Arsenal. Extending the view to Gameweeks 1-5 killed that idea: Arsenal hold the worst run of any strong side at 3.20 average difficulty - Coventry at home, then Villa away, Chelsea at home, Sunderland away and Brighton away. A squad built on them scores well once and then needs -4 hits to repair.

The best runs belong to Liverpool at 2.60, then Manchester City, Manchester United and Leeds at 2.80. No club now contributes more than two players, so the squad can absorb a bad fixture without forced surgery.

Fading a 69% owned forward

Haaland was left out. On projected points the two builds are level, so the tiebreak is structure: £15.5m on one player forces cheap filler elsewhere, and the Haaland build concentrated Arsenal assets into that poor run. Bruno Fernandes costs £3.5m less and carries the higher Gameweek 2-5 projection of the two.

The risk is stated plainly rather than buried. Haaland is owned by 69.4% of managers and captained by many of them. If he returns against Bournemouth this squad loses ground to most of the field in week one. That is a rank cost accepted in exchange for a points structure that holds for five weeks.
