03 Chips

The first-half wildcard was played, as committed in writing last gameweek. Seven of the eight chip slots remain, and the three other first-half chips still have to be placed by Gameweek 19. The case had only got stronger over the break. Last week four of fifteen were dead; this week it was still four, with Scott's thigh injury replacing João Pedro's knee. Obi remains on loan in the Netherlands, Thiago had 10 points from five gameweeks, Dubravka had not played a minute. That is roughly £24m of a £98.4m squad producing nothing, and it is why Haaland could not be bought any other way: no combination of two sales from that squad reached £15.6m while leaving a legal forward line. Only one chip can be played per gameweek, so Bench Boost, Triple Captain and Free Hit were never on the table this week. But the wildcard changes their prospects. The bench is now Verbruggen, Davis, Janelt and Calvert-Lewin, four players who start for their clubs, which is the first time this season Bench Boost has been worth more than about six points. Triple Captain is waiting for Haaland at home to a promoted side. Free Hit stays in the drawer: there are no blank gameweeks before Gameweek 19 and it should not be spent on an ordinary week. The squad was built as an exact optimisation rather than by hand: every eligible player was rated for this gameweek plus a 0.4-weighted projection for Gameweeks 7 to 10, and a mixed-integer solver chose the 15 that maximise the best legal eleven plus a small weight on the bench, under the £99.5m budget, the position quotas and the three-per-club limit. The same solver was then re-run six ways: without Bogle, without Cherki, without both, without João Pedro, and with the horizon weighted at 0.25 and 0.6. Nine of the eleven never changed. That is what made this a squad worth committing a chip to.

06 Decision process

The wildcard, by the numbers

Ten transfers out, ten in, all free. Five players kept: Tarkowski, Groß, Cherki, João Pedro and Verbruggen. Budget was £98.4m of selling value plus £1.1m in the bank, and the new squad costs exactly £99.5m with nothing left over.

The shape of the rebuild: the £11.9m on Fernandes and the £8.0m on Gabriel came out, and with the dead money recycled that bought Haaland at £15.6m and Saka at £9.6m. The spine of the defence is now Tarkowski, De Cuyper and Gvardiol, three of the four highest-rated defenders in the game, with Bogle at £4.6m the fourth starter. Every one of the fifteen is a first-choice player at his club, which could not be said of the squad it replaces.

The old squad's best eleven projected 61.0 for this gameweek. The new one projects 75.8.

What was kept, and why

Tarkowski and Groß were never in question: 43 and 47 points respectively, the two highest ratings in their positions, and both confirmed starters. Verbruggen stays as the reserve keeper because he is a starter at £4.5m, which is better bench cover than a £4.0m keeper who never plays.

Cherki and João Pedro are the two judgement calls. Cherki has a live rotation question at City and only 129 of the last 180 minutes, which the rating already discounts, but his four-gameweek projection of 8.4 is the highest of any midfielder in the game. On a wildcard the horizon matters, so he stays. The solver without him loses only 0.5, so if he is benched at Anfield it is not a disaster.

João Pedro has been passed fit and trained, and one outlet still has Welbeck starting. The solver without him loses 0.3. He starts because the bench covers him: if he records zero minutes, the automatic substitution brings on Davis, who starts for Ipswich at home to Fulham, and the side goes to 5-4-1. The upside of an 8.2-a-game forward at home to Bournemouth costs nothing to carry.

Three City players at Anfield, and two Leeds players at the Emirates

The concentration risk is real and deliberate. Haaland, Gvardiol and Cherki all go to Liverpool at difficulty 4. Liverpool are missing Isak and Gakpo and sweating on Jacquet, and City are without Foden, so neither side is at full strength. If City are beaten comfortably, three of the eleven blank together.

Bogle and Calvert-Lewin both play at Arsenal, rated difficulty 5 for Leeds. Bogle starts, Calvert-Lewin is fourth on the bench. Bogle is in the side for his 8.4 points a game as a wing-back and because at £4.6m he is the player who lets the budget close with Saka and Haaland both in it. The solver without him swaps in Hall and downgrades Saka to Cunha to pay for it, losing a point. For this one gameweek his projection and Hall's are level at 6.0; after it, Leeds' fixtures ease.

Both risks were chosen over the alternatives on the numbers. They are listed here so nobody mistakes them for oversights.

Every one of the fifteen checked against the press

All twenty clubs' Friday news was read, and every club with a player in this squad was checked individually. All eleven starters appear in their club's predicted eleven: Raya and Saka with Havertz out and Konsa doubtful; Tarkowski behind Pickford at Hull; De Cuyper and Groß with Dunk back in training; Gvardiol, Cherki and Haaland, Haaland passed fit after coming off early for Norway; Bogle at wing-back in a 3-5-2; Schade with Damsgaard injured; João Pedro passed fit.

The bench was checked to the same standard, because a bench that does not play is what made Bench Boost unplayable for five weeks. Davis has started every Ipswich match. Janelt is in Brentford's eleven. Calvert-Lewin partners Okafor for Leeds.

Two things the API had not caught up with and the press had: Neco Williams' fever, which made the 50% flag a sale rather than a hold, and the Cherki-or-Bouaddi question at City, which is nowhere in the data.

05 What this model cannot see

  • This is the first chip ever played through this code. The payload was dry-run and inspected, the API accepted it, and the squad read back with the wildcard showing as active and no transfer cost. It is verified, but it was verified for the first time three hours before a deadline.
  • Not captaining a 74%-owned Haaland is the single largest rank risk this squad has taken. It is taken on a projection, and projections at Anfield have a wide error.
  • Groß's rating rests on two consecutive hauls of 12 and 19. Two is more than one, but it is still two. His fixture-only projection is 6.6; the rest is form.
  • Squad value is £99.9m on purchase prices and will read lower on selling prices from next week, because every new signing sells at a 50% loss on any rise. The wildcard crystallised about £1.3m of drift from the opening £100.0m.
  • The squad is optimised for Gameweeks 6 to 10 as a block. Three of the new signings, Bogle, Calvert-Lewin and Schade, play for clubs whose fixtures turn harder after Gameweek 8, so the free transfers that accumulate from here have known targets.
