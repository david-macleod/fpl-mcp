06 Decision process

This one was actually executed

The transfers are made and the line-up is saved. Both were applied to the live team roughly three hours before the 18:30 UK deadline and read back from the API to confirm they landed: two transfers made against a limit of two, so no points hit, and Bruno Fernandes carrying the multiplier.

This matters because Gameweek 2 was lost to exactly this gap. The plan was published four days early and never applied, and the deadline passed with the Gameweek 1 side still in place - 18 points were left on the bench, most of them Tarkowski's. The squad still scored 104 and climbed from 5.28 million to 1.87 million overall, which is the luckiest possible outcome of doing nothing.

Two free transfers, spent on the two weakest links

Both free transfers were used. Neither was spent on the worst players in the squad, because the worst players in the squad are bench fodder and bench fodder does not score points. They were spent on the two weakest members of the starting eleven.

Rice was the clearer of the two. Eight points, 67 and 84 minutes, taken off in both matches, and £7.5m of a £100m budget tied up in a midfielder Arsenal keep substituting. Selling him funded all but £0.2m of Cherki.

The keeper move is what made the sums work. Roefs to Verbruggen is worth about 0.8 projected points on its own, which would not normally justify a transfer, but it also released the £0.5m without which Cherki was unaffordable at £0.0m in the bank. The two moves are one decision, not two.

Haaland was checked and is out of reach

The single biggest lever in the game this week is Erling Haaland at home to Coventry, projected 8.9 and owned by 72% of the field. This squad cannot get him.

At £15.5m he needs the whole of Thiago (£8.0m) plus João Pedro (£7.6m) to fund, which would leave one forward in a squad that must carry three. Selling Thiago and Obi raises £12.5m and falls £3.0m short. There is no two-transfer path to Haaland from £0.0m in the bank, and the third transfer would be a -4 on top.

Cherki at £7.7m is the way into that Manchester City attack at this budget, and it takes the squad to the maximum three City players alongside Guéhi and Anderson. Getting to Haaland properly is a Wildcard job.

Tarkowski starts, Wilson drops, and the model lost that argument

The projection model preferred Wilson (LEE) at 5.2 over Tarkowski (EVE) at 4.8, because Everton host Manchester United at difficulty 4 and defenders get punished hardest by a difficult fixture. The model was overruled.

Tarkowski has 18 points from 180 minutes and has not been substituted. Wilson has 4 points from 110 minutes, was hooked at half-time last week, and is described in Leeds previews as pushing for a return to the side rather than holding a place in it. Blending points-per-game with form puts Tarkowski at 9.0 and Wilson at 2.0 - a seven-point gap the fixture-difficulty adjustment cannot cover.

The side therefore lines up 4-4-2 rather than 3-5-2. Wilson drops to the bench behind Neco Williams, who is worth less per match but plays all 90 minutes every week - and a substitute only pays out if he actually played.

Every club was checked against the press, not the API

All fifteen players were verified against Friday's predicted line-ups and press conference round-ups rather than the API's availability fields, which lag manager quotes by hours. All eleven starters appear in their club's predicted XI.

Two things the API had no idea about. Manchester City signed Enzo Fernández (£125m) and Iliman Ndiaye (£65m) on deadline day; Fernández is expected to come straight in, which is a live threat to Elliot Anderson's place in the pivot and the main risk carried into this gameweek. And Matheus Nunes, whom the model ranked as the third-best defensive buy in the game at 6.4 projected points, is carrying a muscle injury and was quietly discarded - a transfer the data alone would have recommended.

05 What this model cannot see

  • Three Manchester City players now sit in the eleven - Guéhi, Cherki and Anderson - which is the maximum the rules allow and a genuine concentration risk. If City are held at home by Coventry, a quarter of the side fails at once.
  • Cherki's start is taken from predicted line-ups, not a team sheet. Enzo Fernández arriving for £125m makes City's midfield the least predictable in the league this week, and the deadline falls before any of it is confirmed.
  • Thiago keeps his place on fixture and minutes alone. He has 2 points from 172 minutes and is the weakest starter in the side; there was simply no forward available under £8.0m projecting better this week.
  • Projected points are this model's estimate, not a forecast anyone should bet on. The model blends last season's rate with this season's, and after two gameweeks the sample it is working from is very thin.
